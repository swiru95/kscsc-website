Governance, Risk & Compliance

Security policies scoped to what your team actually controls, and written so engineers can follow them.

Most policy templates are written for a whole organisation. Product and platform teams rarely are one. They sit inside a larger group that already owns HR, legal, corporate IT and enterprise risk, and still have to answer customer security questionnaires and pass audits for the product they build and run.

We write policy frameworks for that boundary. Each policy states what is inherited from group policy, what the team owns outright, and where responsibility is shared with customers, the parent company or the cloud provider. Requirements are aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022, SOC 2 and, for industrial products, IEC 62443-4-1.

Every policy names concrete mechanisms rather than aspirations: just-in-time privileged access, customer-managed keys, immutable backups, infrastructure-as-code-only production, and SAST/DAST gates in the pipeline. Each one ends with how compliance is measured and what evidence an auditor will be shown.

What you receive

A policy suite scoped to your remit

Policies cover what your team controls and defer to group policy for the rest. Nothing is duplicated, and nothing falls into the gap between the two.

Shared responsibility made explicit

For each layer (devices, gateways, cloud), who grants access, who builds the mechanism, and who is accountable. The customer, the parent company and the cloud provider each get their share.

Controls engineers can implement

Named mechanisms such as Entra PIM, managed identities, customer-managed keys, WORM backups and CodeQL, ZAP and Dependabot gates. Engineers can turn a requirement into a ticket.

Measurable compliance and evidence

Every policy closes with how compliance is verified and which artefacts prove it, such as drill reports, attendance records, supplier security proofs and release-gate sign-offs.

Framework alignment

Each policy maps to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls and SOC 2 criteria. That mapping is what customer security questionnaires and certification audits ask for.

Operating rhythm, not shelfware

Review cadences, remediation SLAs, KPIs and the triggers that tell you when a policy needs to change. The framework keeps working after the documents are signed.

Sample policies

Context. This framework was written for the development team of an industrial IoT platform (sensors, gateways and Azure cloud services) operating inside a larger enterprise group. It covers the areas the team controlled and defers to group policy for business-wide matters, so it is deliberately not a complete ISMS on its own. Organisation names are replaced with [Company], [Division] and [Parent Company].

IS01 — Information Security Policy

Framework root 5 pages ISO 27001 5.1

The top of the framework: management commitment, CIA objectives, the index of the eleven supporting policies, roles, management-review oversight, and how exceptions and non-compliance are handled.

View PDF See control mapping

DP01 — Data Protection & Retention Policy

4 pages GDPR

Four-tier data classification with an encryption, MFA and sharing matrix. Bans production data from non-production environments, prefers customer-managed keys, and sets a retention schedule from logs to post-contract customer data.

View PDF

IS02 — Access Control Policy

8 pages Zero Trust

Splits access responsibility across sensor, gateway and cloud layers. Requires one identity per person, device and service, managed identities, and Entra PIM for just-in-time access. Break-glass accounts get offline credentials, sign-in alerting and 90-day tests.

View PDF

IS03 — Asset Management Policy

3 pages Supplements group policy

A named owner for every platform asset, personal accountability for assigned equipment, and acceptable and prohibited use. It adds platform-specific rules and leaves group asset policy in place.

View PDF

IS04 — Security Awareness & Training Policy

3 pages Biweekly cadence

Biweekly awareness sessions led by the security team and email advisories for threats seen in the wild, layered on top of mandatory corporate training. Attendance and completion records serve as evidence.

View PDF

IS05 — Backup Policy

3 pages Ransomware-resilient

Daily, weekly and monthly backups of critical data. IaC state is backed up on every change, copies are geo-separated, storage is WORM where supported, encryption uses customer-managed keys, and restoration drills are mandatory.

View PDF

IS06 — Third-Party Supplier Policy

3 pages Supply chain

Suppliers must show security proof before onboarding: pentest reports, policies, ISO 27001 or SOC 2. Vendor firmware is tested internally before release, remediation SLAs go into contracts, and the security team can veto a supplier.

View PDF

IS07 — Logging & Monitoring Policy

3 pages SLA monitoring

An audit trail for every operation on customer data, gateway logs centralised and shared with clients by contract, and SLA alerting. Data is pseudonymised before BI use, and logs are protected against tampering.

View PDF

IS08 — Secure Development Policy (DevSecOps)

3 pages IEC 62443-4-1

Code moves Dev → Beta → Production, and the security team reviews user stories before Beta. Every repository gets secret and dependency scanning, SAST runs in development and DAST on Beta. Deployment is CI/CD-only with least-privilege service principals.

View PDF

IS09 — Vulnerability Management Policy

4 pages Remediation SLAs

Configurations are checked against CIS and NIST, CVE and CWE. Internal pentests run every two release cycles, an independent test runs each year, and customers can audit under NDA. Criticals are fixed within 48 hours and highs within two weeks.

View PDF

IS10 — Cloud Service Security Policy

4 pages IaC-only production

Only vetted, contracted cloud providers may be used. Production and Beta are managed exclusively through infrastructure as code with no manual changes, management-plane activity is monitored, and every resource has a tagged owner.

View PDF

IS10 Appendix A — Azure Cloud Security Governance

5 pages Microsoft CAF

A Security Baseline discipline built on the Cloud Adoption Framework. It sets out policy statements, a business-risk register, metrics, and the triggers that force a policy change. It also covers enforcement actions and the Azure toolchain: Bicep, GitHub Actions, Sentinel and Defender for Cloud.
